title: Neglected Dreams
---

There's a few interest I'm realizing that have quietly followed me for years. And if I had only one year left to live, these might be places I would start with. I want to document them openly and honestly. I want to live closer to what matters to me — little by little.

**Model Airplanes**
I’ve always been captivated by aircraft — by the elegance of flight and the puzzle of why it works. I want to learn how to design a model airplane from first principles, using optimization and math to describe what makes one fly well.

**Conversations and Essays**
People often tell me I ask good questions. I love the way a conversation can challenge my assumptions and create new tensions in how I see the world. I’d like to capture that — to write pieces based on interviews that explore what those exchanges mean to me. Writing has always been how I make sense of things. I miss the process of thinking deeply, revising, and shaping ideas into something beautiful. Some of my happiest memories come from high school literature classes — discussing books, analyzing passages, surprising ourselves with interpretations. I want that again: to write, to analyze, and to share ideas with others who care enough to go deep.

**Behavioral Economics, Decision Science, and Climate Change**
I keep circling back to how behavioral science might help address large-scale problems like climate change. I’m not convinced information is our main bottleneck — maybe it’s insight, prioritization, or organization. Personal action feels insufficient; what I want is to understand how to influence systems, policy, and collective behavior at scale.

**Fact-Based Writing for the Public**
I like learning about new topics and explaining them clearly. I’d love to create accessible write-ups on issues like infrastructure, education, inequality, and migration — ideally grounded in data or math modeling. Part of why I studied statistics was to better evaluate research; I haven’t done enough of that yet, but I’d like to start.

**Poetry**
I miss poetry — memorizing it, performing it, sitting with language until it feels like music. I want to read more of it again.

**Baking**
Baking feels like home. It’s love made tangible — the choice between boredom and creation. It’s memory, generosity, and sensory joy all at once. Plus, it’s delicious and affordable. Someday, maybe, a bakery.

**Guitar**
When I play well, I get chills. When I listen to great music, the same. Isn't that reason enough to keep playing? 

**Modeling Ecosystems**
I’m fascinated by how we might describe ecosystems mathematically — how to reforest optimally, how nutrients flow, how diversity sustains itself. Agroecology and agricultural reform seem like essential problems for climate change. I’d love to model those dynamics — soil, water, population, symbiosis — and see how far you can simulate a living system.

**Quantifying the Human**
Math hits a wall when it comes to feelings and ethics. But I keep wondering: can we model interpersonal dilemmas? Could tools like game theory or natural language processing help us approach moral questions quantitatively? I want to understand prosocial behavior, to use whatever tools I have to figure out what it means to be good.

**Community**
Growing up, I loved my neighborhood — kids running through the woods, families who actually knew each other, the kind of place where you could borrow a cup of sugar. I want to help rebuild that kind of social capital: clubs, local art, small festivals, real connection. Maybe I’ll start with my academic department.
