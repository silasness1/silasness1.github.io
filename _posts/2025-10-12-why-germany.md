Education here is affordable, rigorous, and refreshingly international. German universities, in my experience, demand depth and independence—qualities I value.

Living abroad also gives me a chance to redefine myself. Being an international student has a kind of monastic focus: fewer distractions, more room for study and reflection.

And practically, I’ve already been learning German; continuing to deepen my language skills feels like the natural next step. Keeping one foot in both cultures—American and German—feels like both a safeguard and a source of perspective.

But I wasn't just choosing to go to Germany in isolation, I was also picking a path forward in my education. Below, I reflect on some of my intentions for the next two years.

### Grad School for Econometrics

1) Critical Thinking in Quantitative Modeling
I want to be able to read, understand, and critique empirical research across many fields. That means learning to:
- Identify the assumptions behind models and where they break down in real-world contexts.
- Build a broad quantitative toolkit that helps me analyze a variety of problems.
- Opportunites: journal reading club, office hours, create a literal toolbox of flashcards with modeling techniques

2) Creative Inquiry, Problem Framing, and Real World Synthesis
Econometrics isn’t just about applying methods—it’s about framing messy, ambiguous problems in a way that makes them analyzable. I want to:
- Cultivate curiousity to discover new applications.
- Practice creating simplifying assumptions and meaningful proxies.
- Opportunities: keep a running list of real world messy problems, learn to reverse-engineer abstracts to uncover their underlying research questions, connect people and ideas across disciplines, pitching ideas to professors even if I plan to carry out the research project

3) Tool Selection and Technical Fluency
A good analyst knows not just how to use tools, but when each one fits the structure of a problem. I want to:
- Program end-to-end projects from inspiration to interpretation.
- Develop a "conceptual vocabulary" for domain-specific applications
- Build intution for choosing the righ tool for each task.
- Opportunites: hackathons, maintaining a tool diary to reflect on my choices and learn from them

4) Professional Visibility & Career Capital
I’m not doing this degree in isolation—I want my work to be visible and connected to real-world impact. That includes:
- Building a public facing portfolio through a personal blog and Github oresence
- Developing professional fluency in German
- Pursuing coauthorships, internships, and publishable projects
- Leveraging course projects as opportunities for growth and visibility
